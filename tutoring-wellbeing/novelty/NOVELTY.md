# Prior-work check

**Question checked:** Has anyone already analysed the association between additional/private (shadow) mathematics instruction and student life satisfaction or wellbeing across countries in PISA 2022?

**Answer, with limits:** No such study was found. The closest are single-system studies (Hong Kong PISA 2018; Taiwan; Chinese and Korean panels) and one PISA 2022 cross-national study whose outcome is mathematics achievement, not wellbeing.

## Limits of this check (state these honestly)

- OpenCitations covers only works whose references are openly deposited. For example, it found 7 citing works for Karakus 2024, while OpenAlex counts 13. **Some citing works were missed.**
- OpenAlex and Semantic Scholar refused requests from this environment (shared-IP rate limits). Scopus, Web of Science, Google Scholar, ERIC and PsycINFO were not searched. **Before submission, rerun the forward-citation scan of the 7 anchors in Google Scholar or Scopus, and search ERIC and PsycINFO.**
- 233 of 419 citing works had no abstract in Crossref, so they were screened on title only.
- Novelty is a claim about what has been published, so it can go out of date. Recheck immediately before submission.

## What makes the question worth publishing

1. **Scale:** 72 education systems and about half a million students, against single-system evidence so far.
2. **Forms:** first chance to compare one-on-one, online, video and group formats against wellbeing.
3. **Context (E3):** tests whether the association depends on how normal tutoring is in a system, a question single-country studies can't answer.
4. **Policy relevance:** China's 2021 "double reduction" policy and similar regulation elsewhere are justified partly by student wellbeing, yet cross-national evidence has been missing.
